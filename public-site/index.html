Failed experiments

  • Completed-work invoice exception audit for commercial tree-care contractors - a fixed-price pilot comparing completed job records against invoices to find work that was done but never billed.

    We struggled to reach the person responsible for invoicing. Early outreach relied too heavily on general enquiry inboxes and small firms; a narrower retest ran into bounced emails. We did not establish paid demand and stopped allocating time to it.

  • PAT register rescue for testing businesses - a fixed-price pilot cleaning up an existing appliance register, with an import-ready file and an exception list for duplicates, blanks and conflicting dates.

    Search ads attracted software shoppers rather than buyers of a one-off cleanup, and existing tools already handled routine imports. Landing-page errors and poor contact data weakened the tests. We never established a costly cleanup problem buyers would pay us to fix.

  • Delivery payout reconciliation for restaurant accounting firms - a fixed-price manual pilot that turns one client's delivery-platform exports into a review-ready posting pack and a source-linked exception report.

    We changed a restaurant-automation idea into a manual service for accountants without validating that buyer. Outreach produced no positive replies, while poor targeting and contact data weakened the test. We did not establish paid demand and parked the offer.

  • Outoffer (customer-acquisition marketplace)

    Recruiting merchants would require burdensome outreach to the wrong customer profile. We judged the chance of success too low to justify the time, so the opportunity cost ruled it out.

  • Voxpopli (privacy-first open source microblogging) - not a good business model for bootstrapping; community building is extremely challenging; difficult to get network effects even with good supply-side for founders.
  • Popper (polymarket for falsification - conjectures and refutations web app with Ethereum bounty staking) - too esoteric and difficult to get in front of potential buyers (institutional, risk-averse); not an ideal business model for bootstrapping. Potential effective altruism-aligned funders not interested.
